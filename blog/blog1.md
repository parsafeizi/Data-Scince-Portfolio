What is Data Science to Me?
To me, data science is the process of turning raw data into meaningful information that an individual or organization can use to make better decisions. It involves collecting, organizing, and analyzing data to identify patterns and better understand a particular topic. Although data science is commonly used in business, it can also help us understand important social issues and human behavior.

Learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts matters because numbers often represent real people and their experiences. For example, data can be used to study crime rates, access to healthcare, education, employment, poverty, and many other social topics. Modeling these subjects can help researchers and decision-makers identify patterns that might otherwise go unnoticed. It can also help them understand which communities may need additional resources or support. When used correctly, data science can provide evidence that helps organizations and governments create better policies and solutions.

However, using data about people also comes with important responsibilities. One responsibility is protecting people’s privacy and making sure that personal information is not shared or misused. Data scientists must also consider whether the data they use is accurate, complete, and representative of the population being studied. If a dataset is biased or leaves out certain groups, the conclusions made from it may also be biased. This could lead to unfair decisions that negatively affect real people. Therefore, data scientists should be honest about the limitations of their data and avoid presenting their results as completely certain when uncertainty exists.

Another responsibility is remembering that people are more complicated than numbers in a dataset. A model might show a relationship between two variables, but it may not fully explain why that relationship exists. Social, cultural, historical, and economic factors can all influence human behavior. For this reason, it is important to combine data science with knowledge from other subjects instead of relying only on statistics or computer models.
